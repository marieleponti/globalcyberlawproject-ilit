Countermeasures

Questions
    • 1. Are countermeasures a part of customary international law?

    • 2. Can cyber operations be employed as countermeasures?

      • 2a. May a state take cyber countermeasures in response to non-cyber related breaches of international law?

      • 2b. May a state take non-cyber countermeasures in response to a cyber operation that constitutes an internationally wrongful act?

    • 3. May countermeasures be taken only for the purpose of inducing a responsible state to comply with its international obligations?

    • 4. May countermeasures directly achieve compliance by the targeted state with its international legal obligation?

    • 5. Can there be other purposes for countermeasures i.e., punishment?

    • 6. Must a state exhaust lesser means of inducement prior to resorting to countermeasures?

    • 7. Must the target of countermeasures be state organs or infrastructure?

    • 8. May countermeasures be taken against non-state actors?

    • 9. May a countermeasure against a responsible state involve the breach of an international legal obligation owed to a third state or other party?

      • 9a. Is there a de minimis exception where countermeasures impact third parties or third states' international rights?

    • 10. May countermeasures involve the use of force?

      • 10a. If the original intentionally wrongful act is a use of force that does not constitute an armed attack, can a state's responsive countermeasures involve a proportionate use of force that also does not rise to the level of an armed attack?

    • 11. Are the wrongfulness of measures characterized as countermeasures precluded if taken based on a reasonable mistake that the targeted state was in breach of an applicable international legal obligation?

    • 12. May countermeasures be taken in anticipation of an internationally wrongful act?

    • 13. Are countermeasures precluded if the precedent internationally wrongful act has ceased, is unlikely to be repeated, and assurances or guarantees, where appropriate have been provided?

    • 14. Must countermeasures be reversible in their effects?

      • 14a. If yes, is this requirement subject to feasibility?

      • 14b. If yes, must a state considering the employment of countermeasures opt for the most reversible measure?

    • 15. May a state take countermeasures if the dispute in question is pending before a court or tribunal with jurisdiction to issue a binding decision in the matter and the internationally wrongful act has ceased?

    • 16. May countermeasures involve the violation of peremptory norms of international law?

    • 17. May countermeasures involve the violation of non-peremptory, "fundamental" human rights?

    • 18. May countermeasures be taken that amount to belligerent reprisals?

    • 19. May countermeasures infringe on diplomatic or consular inviolability?

    • 20. Can states resort to collective countermeasures?

      • 20a. Generally?

      • 20b. Only in a cyber context?

    • 21. May a non-injured state provide assistance to an injured state's countermeasures?

    • 22. May a state party to a multilateral treaty breached by a responsibly state take countermeasures if it is not directly injured by the breach?

    • 23. May a state take countermeasures against a breach of an erga omnes obligation if not directly injured?

    • 24. Are there procedural and substantive conditions that must be met before countermeasures can be taken?

      • 24a. Must an injured state first call upon the responsible state to cease its internationally wrongful act and/or fulfil its obligations arising from its breach prior to employing countermeasures?

        • 24a.i. Is this procedural requirement absolute?

      • 24b. Must an injured state notify the responsible state of its intent to employ countermeasures in order to give the responsible state a chance to cease its wrongful actions prior to taking countermeasures?

        • 24b.i. Is this procedural requirement absolute?

      • 24c. Must an injured state offer to negotiate with the responsible state prior to taking countermeasures?

        • 24c.i. May an injured state take countermeasures while in negotiations with the responsible state?

      • 24d. Must countermeasures in cyber be proportional to the need to return the responsible state to compliance with international law?
