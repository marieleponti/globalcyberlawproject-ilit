Sovereignty

Questions
    • 1. Is sovereignty a foundational principle of the international legal order?

    • 2. Does sovereignty confer on states authority over cyber infrastructure located in their respective territories and over the actions they take with respect to that infrastructure?

      • 2.1 Does sovereignty encompass the right of a State to protect cyber infrastructure and safeguard cyber activity that is located in, or takes place on, its territory?

      • 2.2 Does sovereignty encompass the right of a State to control government data stored or transmitted inside its territory?

      • 2.3 Does sovereignty encompass the right of a State to control data of its nationals stored or transmitted inside its territory?

      • 2.4 Does sovereignty encompass the right of a State to control government data stored or transmitted outside their its territory?

      • 2.5 Does sovereignty encompass the right of a State to control data of its nationals stored or transmitted outside its territory?

      • 2.6 Is the exercise of such sovereign authority subject to international human rights law and other fundamental freedoms?

    • 3. Is sovereignty a stand-alone, primary rule of international law, the breach of which can incur international responsibility?

      • 3.1 Can violations of sovereignty not amounting to prohibited interventions constitute internationally wrongful acts?

    • 4. Does the State indicate how to identify a violation of any stand-alone sovereignty rule?

    • 5. Is it a violation of territorial sovereignty for an organ of a State, or others whose conduct is attributable to the State, to conduct non-consensual cyber operations while physically present on another State’s territory against that State or entities or persons on its territory?

    • 6. Can state cyber operations that cause physical effects in another state’s territory constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

      • 6.1 Do any physical effects constitute a violation?

      • 6.2 Must the effects involve physical damage to computer infrastructure or other tangible things, or injury to persons, to constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

        • 6.2.i. Must the effect of a cyber operation to computer infrastructure require repair or replacement of physical components to qualify as damage?
      • 6.3 Do only cyber operations generating significant physical effects within another State’s territory constitute a violation?

    • 7. Can state cyber operations that cause a loss to computer functionality in another State’s territory constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

      • 7.1 Is the rule violated by a cyber operation generating any functionality losses within another state’s territory?

      • 7.2 Is the rule violated by a cyber operation generating significant functionality losses within another state’s territory?

      • 7.3 Is the rule violated by a cyber operation generating systemic functionality losses within another state’s territory?

    • 8. Can state cyber operations that interfere with another state’s “inherent governmental functions” constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

    • 9. Must there be proximate causation between the state cyber operation and the effects or interference prohibited by sovereignty as a rule?

      • 9.1 Must the state cyber operation generate prohibited effects directly?

      • 9.2 Do prohibited effects indirectly generated by a state cyber operation constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

    • 10. Can state cyber operations that deny access to or degrade data in the control of another State constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

      • 10.1 Do state sponsored ransomware operations violate the sovereignty of another State?

      • 10.2 Can state cyber operations against data residing in cloud servers outside of a State’s territorial jurisdiction constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

      • 10.3 Does the exercise of one State’s law enforcement tools via cyber means in the networks or systems of another State’s territory violate the sovereignty of the latter State?

    • 11. Do State cyber operations that interfere at any level with critical infrastructure in another State constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

    • 12. Can State cyber operations that cause significant national economic impacts for another State constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

    • 13. Does cyber espionage by one State in or transiting networks or hardware resident in another State constitute a violation of sovereignty as a rule?

      • 13.1 Can gaining unauthorized access to computer systems or data within a State’s sovereign control violate the rules of sovereignty?

    • 14. Are violations of sovereignty assessed on a case-by-case basis?
